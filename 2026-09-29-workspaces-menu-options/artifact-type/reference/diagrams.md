# Diagrams: flowcharts, org charts, system maps, swimlanes, sequence diagrams, timelines

**Build a diagram from separate elements, never as one `<svg>`.** The editor selects and drags an `<svg>` as one block, so in a flowchart drawn as one, nobody can move or retype a single box. Guidance you may have loaded to hand-draw a diagram as inline `<svg>` (in a `<figure>`) is for ordinary pages, not a canvas. One `<svg>` is still right where nobody will move or retype a part: an icon, a logo, an illustration, a data chart's plot (labels and callouts a person will retype sit over it as separate elements). Steps in a row inside a page, joined only by chevrons, stay a flex row.

## The host

A diagram is the one exception to the flex-or-grid rule: an arrow's numbers come from its cards' px positions, and a flex child has none. Everything sits in ONE host, a `<div>` with `position: relative` and a px `width` and `height`, each element placed in it by inline `position: absolute; left; top` in px. When the diagram is the whole artboard, the host IS the artboard's root element and the title is one more placed element (every card and arrow is then one click away); keep every element 64px inside the root's edges, since padding does not move placed elements.

Write every element out as literal markup: a card made by `<sc-for>`, or whose `left`/`top` is a `{{hole}}`, cannot be dragged alone (a click selects every copy). File order is paint order and click order, and where two boxes overlap the later one takes the click: background bands first, then every arrow, then cards, then labels.

## The elements

- **Card**: a `<div>` with inline `width`, `height` and `box-sizing: border-box` (the arrow formulas need outer sizes), its label inside it as text and inline tags only (`<b>`, `<span>`, `<br>`), never child `<div>`s: then one click selects the card and a drag moves card and label as one (a block child gets pulled out of the card instead). The card is a flex box, so a label with a tag in it goes in ONE `<span>`, as in the example; loose, its parts sit side by side and lose their spaces. An icon goes inside its card, before that `<span>`, with `gap: 8px` on the card (its words then select alone; the card drags by its icon or padding). Size each kind of card for its longest label once wrapped (at 16px about 9px a character, 11 in capitals, and 24px a line, plus padding), in even numbers; if the diagram does not fit, make the artboard bigger, never the text smaller. A decision diamond is a card about twice as wide and as tall as its label, with `clip-path: polygon(50% 0, 100% 50%, 50% 100%, 0 50%)` and a fill, not a border (the clip cuts one off); its four points are the middles of the card's edges. Never a rotated square.
- **Arrow**: one small `<svg>` per STRAIGHT segment, copied from the example with every attribute. It is the svg the editor's own Arrow tool draws, with a filled head under a marker `id` of its own (arrows a person draws with the tool have an open head): it selects and drags alone and shows a handle at each end. Colour, width and dashes go in the svg's `style` (`stroke`, `stroke-width`, `stroke-dasharray: 6 6`), as the tool writes them; the head takes the arrow's colour and width by itself and is never dashed (in Safari the colour does not follow: Safari paints the `fill` attribute on the head's own path, so give that the arrow's colour). The Properties panel can write a person's changes as `stroke` and `stroke-width` attributes on the arrow's `<path>`, and those win: restyle such an arrow there. Every arrow repeats the same `<defs>`, `id` included (the editor's own arrows repeat theirs too), so that deleting or duplicating one never removes another arrow's head: no shared `<defs>`, no numbered ids, no other change to the head. A plain line is the same svg without `<defs>` and `marker-end`.
- **Bent arrow**: a chain of straight segments, each starting exactly where the last ended, the head on the last only (one path with several points, or a curve, is selected as a box, and resizing stretches it).
- **One to many**: a line out of the parent to the middle of the gap, a line across there from the first child's centre x to the last's, then an arrow from it into each child (in an org chart a plain line, ending ON the edge). Many to one is the mirror image.
- **Label on an arrow** ("yes", "retry"): its own small `<div>`, `font-size: 13px; line-height: 16px`, 8px or more clear of every line and card: above a line at y its `top` is y − 28, below it y + 12; beside a vertical line at x its `left` is x + 12 and its `top` the segment's middle − 8. A "yes" or "no" sits at its decision's end of the segment. Its box takes clicks too, so keep it no wider than its text: `width` just over the text (about 8px a character), `text-align: center`, `left` = the middle of the gap it sits in minus half the width.
- **Lane or other background band**: an empty `<div>`, written first, its name a separate label in a corner, clear of every line and card. Lifelines and a timeline's rail are plain lines.

## The numbers

Settle every card's `left`, `top`, `width` and `height` before you write the file, on a grid: cards in a row share a centre y, cards in a column a centre x, gaps of 48px or more (96 or more where a line runs along one; a label's width + 16 where one sits in it), whole px throughout. An arrow leaves the middle of one card's edge and its head stops one `stroke-width` short of the middle of the other's (2px in the example): a right edge's middle is (`left + width`, `top + height/2`), a bottom edge's (`left + width/2`, `top + height`). Place the cards so that each arrow has an edge of its own; two that must share one (there and back between neighbours) sit a third and two thirds along it, at the same y (or x) on both cards (an edge that divides by 3 keeps these whole). A loop back with no gap or empty cell to run through goes round the outside: out of a free edge, along a clear strip 48px beyond the cards, into a free edge of the earlier card. Keep segments horizontal or vertical, through gaps or empty cells, never across a card.

Each svg is exactly as long as its segment and 8px thick, the line along its middle. Take (x, y) as the segment's LEFT end (TOP end if vertical), whichever way it points, and n as its length:

- horizontal: `left: x; top: y − 4`, `width="n" height="8"`, path `M 0 4 L n 4` pointing right, `M n 4 L 0 4` pointing left
- vertical: `left: x − 4; top: y`, `width="8" height="n"`, path `M 4 0 L 4 n` pointing down, `M 4 n L 4 0` pointing up

The `stroke-width` left short comes off the head's end, so the box of an arrow pointing left or up begins that far clear of the card it points at. The `width`/`height` attributes, the `viewBox` and the style's `width`/`height` always carry the same two numbers.

**Arrows do not follow cards.** When you move or resize a card, work out again every arrow that touches it; a person must do the same by hand, so keep bends few.
