# Sharing & collaboration

<Callout type="info">
  Everything on this page is part of the **hosted** app, except
  [cursor chat](#seeing-each-other). The desktop app is single-user: it has no
  sharing, other people’s cursors, or comments.
</Callout>

A canvas is a shared, live space. Everyone in it sees the same frames,
documents and chats, with changes appearing as they
happen. If a teammate asks the agent for something, you watch it stream in
too.

## Sharing a canvas

As the owner, click **Share** in the canvas’s top right, or **Share** in the
canvas menu on the home screen. Invite people by the email address of their
Screenplay account. **People with access** lists everyone; **Remove** takes
someone off, after you confirm.

There are two roles:

- **Owner** can rename, share, and delete the canvas.
- **Collaborators** can do everything else: add repositories and start chats (from the
  sidebar or Canvas settings), chat
  with the agent, and edit the canvas. They can pin the canvas and file it
  into their own folders. Their **Delete** is **Leave**.

There’s no public link sharing. **Copy link** copies the canvas’s address,
but only people with access can open it.

## Seeing each other

- **Cursors.** Everyone’s pointer shows on the canvas with their name, in
  their color. Selections are outlined in that color too.
- **Avatars.** The top right shows who’s here. Click an avatar to **follow**
  that person: your view tracks their viewport as they pan and zoom. Click
  again, or your own avatar, to stop.
- **Cursor chat.** Press <kbd>/</kbd> to type a short message (up to 140
  characters) that floats next to your cursor for everyone to see.
  <kbd>↵</kbd> or <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes it.

In a chat, each message shows the name of whoever sent it, and a
[question](/guides/agent#questions) the agent asked says who answered it.
Commits are made under the name of whoever sent the message, so git history
stays accurate on a shared canvas.

## Comments

Press <kbd>C</kbd> (or pick **Comment** in the toolbar), hover a frame or mockup to
outline the element under the pointer, then click to pin a comment to it. The
comment is anchored to the chat, the route and the element, so its pin
follows the element and shows only while the frame is on that route.

Type your comment and click **Comment** (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>↵</kbd>).

Pins are numbered in the order their threads were started and are
highlighted when unread. Open a pin to see its thread in a card beside the
element: reply (type <kbd>@</kbd> to mention someone), **Resolve** or
**Reopen**, edit your own comments, and use the card’s menu for **Mark as
unread** or **Delete thread**.

The comment button in the top bar shows how many threads are open, with a dot
when some are unread. It opens the **Comments** panel, which groups threads by
frame and route behind **Open**, **Mine**, **Unread** and **Resolved**
filters. <kbd>J</kbd> and <kbd>K</kbd> step through the threads, opening each
one’s card, and <kbd>E</kbd> resolves or reopens it. The eye in the panel’s
header hides the pins. A comment whose frame or element no longer exists is
listed as **Detached**. In [play mode](/guides/play-mode) you see and add the
same threads.

### Send threads to the agent

A comment can go straight to the agent that builds what it’s pinned to. Pick
**Send to agent** from a thread card’s menu, or tick threads in the
**Comments** panel and click **Send N to agent** at its bottom. Only open
threads on a chat whose code is running get a checkbox. Threads for the
same chat go to its agent together, as one message that names each of
them. A thread on a document goes to the chat that wrote it (see
[Documents](/guides/documents)).

Each thread then shows where the agent is with it: **Queued**, **Agent
working**, and finally **Addressed**. When it’s addressed, the agent’s reply
is added to the thread, and the chip names the commit it made, if any. Resolving
the thread is still up to you. If the agent’s turn fails or is stopped, the
thread drops its status so you can send it again.

### Comment on document text

Select text in a [document](/guides/documents) and click **Comment** at the
end of the formatting toolbar. The composer opens at the document’s right
edge with the quoted passage and its line number, and the passage stays
highlighted while you write. Once you post, the passage keeps a highlight and
the thread’s pin sits beside it. Click the highlight or the pin to open the
thread.

With the Comment tool on (<kbd>C</kbd>), you can also drag across a
document’s text without opening it for editing. Letting go opens the same
composer on that passage. Click without dragging to pin a comment to a spot
instead.

## Terminal tabs stay private

[Terminal tabs](/guides/terminals) belong to the person who opened them and
aren’t visible to collaborators.
