Frame Chrome Explorations

Six ways to give a frame browser-like controls (back, forward, route, reload, status, recording a flow) without the names jumping when you select a frame. Every mockup follows your light or dark theme.

Picked · B + C, one toolbar

One bar under the frame

This merges the Safari-style bar and the right-edge strip into one toolbar under the selected frame. From left to right it holds Interact, then back and forward, then the address field, then Knobs and More. The address field holds the route, reload and record. Recording turns the field red, counts screens and swaps record for stop. Status only appears when the preview isn't live. The name label stays put, and it drops its route pill while the bar shows, because the field shows the route.

  • The bar is centred on the frame and stays a fixed width. On a phone frame it's wider than the frame.
  • When the frame's bottom is off screen, the bar stops above the canvas's bottom toolbar, the way Safari's bar stays in view.
  • While you're interacting, the Interacting tag stays right under the frame and the bar sits below it.
  • Tooltips open upward. Menus and the route list open downward, and flip up near the bottom of the window.

What I took from your notes

  • The name must not move. Selecting a frame shouldn't push its label up. In every direction below, the label keeps the spot it has today.
  • The toolbar looked like a pile of controls. I gave it everything, so it read like a slab of equal-weight buttons. Most of these controls are used now and then. They should stay quiet until you reach for them.
  • Controls should match what they do. Create flow makes a new screen each time you navigate, which is basically recording. A record button next to the route says that better than a route icon in a toolbar. Reload belongs to the address, like Safari.
  • Keep it inside the current UX. The vertical strip on the frame's right edge stays; the horizontal slab above the frame was already turned down once in #796.

A

The route pill becomes the address bar Recommended

The label already ends with a route pill. When the frame is selected, that same pill becomes a small address field: back sits in front of it, and reload and record sit inside its right end. The label only grows to the right, so the name never moves. The right-edge strip goes back to Interact, Knobs and More.

Why it works

  • Nothing moves; the pill you already read becomes the thing you edit.
  • Reload and record read as actions on this page, like Safari's reload and a screen recorder's button.
  • The strip gets shorter, not longer.

Costs

  • The label row gets busier when selected. On phone-width frames the name truncates first, then Back hides.
  • At very low zoom the whole label is already hidden, so these controls are too (same as today).
B

A Safari-style bar under the frame

The label stays exactly as it is today. When you select a frame, a floating address bar hangs centred under it, in the same spot as the Interacting tag, like Safari's bottom bar on iPhone. It uses the shared floating toolbar surface.

Why it works

  • The label is untouched in every state.
  • There's room for the full route, and the bar can stay a fixed width.

Costs

  • The route shows twice (pill and bar) unless the pill hides on selection.
  • It lands on the frame below when frames are stacked tight, and it competes with the Interacting tag.
  • On a phone frame it's wider than the frame.
C

Create flow as recording

This works with A or B. The record button starts Create flow. While it runs, the field turns red and shows a counter, and the button becomes stop, so the mode is hard to miss. Each page you visit leaves a numbered screen behind, just as Create flow does today. The selection ring stays the normal selection colour; only the field changes.

Why it works

  • "Record" explains Create flow better than a route icon with a tooltip.
  • The recording state sits right where the route changes, so you see each step get counted.

Costs

  • Red is loud, so it should only show while recording.
  • Calling it "Record flow" in the tooltip means renaming Create flow in the UI.
D

Grow the right-edge strip

The smallest change. Back, forward and reload join the vertical strip, Create flow becomes a record icon, and the route pill in the label turns into a field only when you click it. Nothing new appears anywhere else.

Why it works

  • No new surfaces. It's today's UI plus three buttons.

Costs

  • The strip grows to eight buttons, taller than a phone frame at low zoom.
  • Back and forward stacked vertically don't read as browser controls.
  • Reload sits far from the route it reloads.
E

An overlay inside the frame

Like Arc's peek: when you hover a selected frame, a translucent address bar slides down inside its top edge. Nothing is drawn outside the frame.

Why it works

  • No chrome outside the frame, so labels and neighbours never collide.

Costs

  • It covers the prototype's own header, which is often what you're designing.
  • It scales with the frame, so it's tiny at low zoom unless counter-scaled.
  • Hover-only controls are hard to discover.
F

Keyboard first, no new chrome

Clicking the route pill (or ⌘L) opens a route palette anchored to it. The palette's footer teaches the shortcuts for back, forward, reload and record. The same actions also go in the More menu. The canvas stays exactly as it looks today.

Why it works

  • Cleanest canvas. Power users get fast keys.

Costs

  • Hard to discover. Nobody finds Back unless they open the palette.
  • Recording has no visible state unless we add one (C).

Where the status dot goes

This applies to any direction. The preview is live almost all the time, so a green dot on every frame is mostly noise. The frame body already shows full-screen states for failed and stopped previews.

My pick

A with C, and status only when it isn't live

The route pill becomes the address field when the frame is selected. Back sits in front of it; Forward shows up only after you've gone back. Reload and record sit at its right end, and recording turns the field red with a count and a stop button. The right-edge strip keeps Interact, Knobs and More. While the preview is live there's no status dot; a spinner or an amber "Disconnected" shows in the field only when something's off.

If A still feels too busy in the label, B is the fallback. It's the same pieces in a bar under the frame. I'd skip D, E and F.
