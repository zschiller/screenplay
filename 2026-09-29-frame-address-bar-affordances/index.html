Frame address bar: Workspace and route

The bar under a selected frame has two parts you can press: the Workspace (like a browser's host) and the route (its path). Today they look alike but answer the pointer differently. Below is what differs now, then three ways to make them agree. Every stage is drawn at app size with the real type, 24px bar and 12px icons; each option shows light then dark.

A · Matched menus

Both parts become the same kind of control: a menu button. Same fill on hover, a caret on each, and both menus drop from the bar's left edge at one width with a search box on top. The route menu stays a list you filter or type a new route into.

  • Good: smallest change; nothing new to learn.
  • Cost: two carets in one field is busy, and typing a route still happens in a box under the route instead of on it.

Dark

B · Pick the Workspace, type the route Recommended

Each part gets the affordance of what it holds, like Safari. The Workspace is picked from a list, so it stays a menu button with its caret and hover card. The route is text, so it behaves as a text field: hover fills it and shows the I-beam, a click selects the route in place, and suggestions drop under the bar at the Workspace menu's width. Enter goes, Esc puts it back.

  • Good: the difference between the parts becomes a rule you can see (caret means menu, I-beam means type), and hover fill, radius and menu position match.
  • Cost: the route field needs real input behaviour (select-all on focus, Esc, blur to cancel) inside the canvas's pointer handling.
Light
Dark

C · One field, one menu

The whole field is a single target. Hover outlines all of it; a click opens one menu with search on top, routes first and Workspaces below. The Workspace keeps its hover card, so hovering still tells you which Workspace you're on.

  • Good: no way for the two parts to disagree; one place to type.
  • Cost: switching Workspace takes a scroll or a search past routes, and it hides the Workspace switch that #867 made one click.
