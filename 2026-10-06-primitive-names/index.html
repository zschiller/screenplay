Primitive Names
Screenplay · naming audit · 6 October 2026

What should we call our primitives?

I compared every primitive a person sees in Screenplay with what design canvases (Figma, Sketch, Penpot, Framer, Miro, tldraw), AI design tools (Google Stitch, Figma Make, Magic Patterns), AI app builders (v0, Lovable) and coding-agent tools (Cursor, Codex, Devin, Conductor) call the same idea.

Short answer: most of our names already match the market. Frame and mockup are the right pair, and “artifact” would be a step backwards. Two things are worth changing: Play mode has two names, and group is the one name a Figma user reads differently.

Each primitive

Canvas

Keep

The container for one piece of work: an infinite surface with its chats, frames, mockups and documents. Code calls it Room.

Google Stitch, Magic Patterns
canvas, in exactly our sense: the infinite surface the AI lays screens out on.
Figma
file (the canvas is the surface inside it)
Miro, FigJam
board
v0, ChatGPT, Claude
project for the container of chats
ChatGPT, Gemini
Canvas means a side-by-side editor for one document or file, a different idea

Why keep: the newest AI design tools converged on “canvas” for this exact thing. “Project” would collide with the GitHub word our users already use, and we deliberately dropped it as a label (#880). The ChatGPT and Gemini meaning is a different product shape and hasn’t confused anyone we know of.

Coordinator

Keep

The canvas’s own chat. It starts chats, sends them work and arranges the canvas, and makes nothing itself.

Microsoft Magentic-One
Orchestrator
LangGraph
supervisor
CrewAI
manager agent
Anthropic research system
lead agent with subagents
Miro, Stitch
Sidekicks, design agent (one assistant that also does the work)

Why keep: every alternative is framework vocabulary that people building agents know and people designing products don’t. “Coordinator” is plain English and says what makes ours different: it delegates instead of doing the work. “Orchestrator” is the only real contender, and it reads as infrastructure.

Chat

Keep

One branch of a repository in its own sandbox, with its own agent, preview and terminals. Code calls it Branch.

v0, ChatGPT, Claude
chat, grouped under a project
Conductor
workspace: a git worktree with its own branch, agent, terminal and PR. The closest match to ours, and the word you already ruled out.
Cursor
agent / cloud agent (renamed from background agent)
OpenAI Codex, Google Jules
task
Devin, Claude Code on the web
session

Why keep: the market splits five ways, so no word is standard. “Task” and “session” suggest something that ends, and ours carries several pull requests over time. “Agent” would blur the chat with the AI inside it. “Chat” is what people start, find and rename, and it matches the most widely used products.

Frame

Keep

A live preview of one route of one chat’s app at a device size. It’s a real browser you can click through.

Figma, Framer
frame: the device-sized rectangle a screen is designed in, with device presets
Sketch
replaced artboards with frames in 2025
Penpot
board (renamed from artboard)
v0, Lovable
preview for the running app
Stitch, Magic Patterns
screen
Miro, tldraw, Excalidraw
frame means a container that groups other things

Why keep: Figma and now Sketch made “frame” the standard name for a screen-sized rectangle on a canvas, so designers read ours correctly at first sight. The iframe echo is a bonus for engineers. The words also split cleanly already: the preview is the running app, and a frame is a window onto it. The whiteboard meaning (a grouping container) is the one risk, and it points at Group below.

  • FramePickThe canvas word for a screen: designers know it from Figma and Sketch, and it sits naturally beside mockup and document.
  • BrowserSays “live and clickable” better than anything else, and the docs already describe frames as “real browsers”. But in agent tools a browser is the agent’s own tool (Cursor’s built-in browser, Devin’s browser tab), a live mockup runs in a browser too, so it doesn’t set frames apart, and canvas verbs get awkward: the Browser tool, “drop a browser”, “3 browsers in this group”. Better kept as the word that explains what a frame is.
  • PreviewWhat v0 and Lovable call the running app, and already our word for it. Using it for the frame too would make “Restart preview” ambiguous.
  • ScreenFits a single page, but one frame clicks through many screens.

On “artifact”

It’s the strongest-known term for AI-made output, which is also the problem. It’s one vendor’s product name, and our copy rules keep that vendor out of the UI. In the tools our users live in, an artifact is a build output (GitHub Actions artifacts, CI artifacts) and shows up next to pull requests every day. And on a canvas, frames and documents are artifacts too, so the word doesn’t tell a mockup apart from anything else.

  • MockupPickSays “a picture of the idea, not the real app”, which is the one thing that has to be clear next to a frame. Slight risk that it sounds static, but the page shows people it’s clickable within seconds.
  • ArtifactVendor-branded, collides with CI artifacts, and doesn’t separate it from frames or documents.
  • PrototypeCollides with the prototype player, which plays the real app, the opposite of a mockup.
  • DesignStitch’s word, but too broad: frames and documents are designs too, and “a design” is awkward in sentences.
  • SketchCollides with the Sketch app and implies drawing by hand.
  • ScreenFits single pages but not a whole flow inside one mockup, and frames are screens too.

Group

Consider Section

A labelled row of frames, documents and mockups laid out left to right. Every layer lives in one, and it names its chat once when all its frames share one.

Figma, FigJam
section: a labelled area that holds frames on the canvas. A Figma group is an unlabelled bundle of layers with no box of its own.
Miro, tldraw, Excalidraw
frame (taken)
Sketch, Framer
stack: an auto-layout row or column
Penpot
nested boards

Why consider: this is the one name a Figma user reads differently. To them a group has no label, no layout and no box, and ours has all three, which is exactly what Figma calls a section. “Stack” describes the row layout but not the label. Renaming touches the canvas, layer menus and docs, so it’s worth doing only if people have actually tripped on it. If not, Group stays.

Document

Keep

A rich-text page on the canvas for specs, checklists, notes and plans.

Miro, Google
Doc
Notion
page
Cursor, Kiro
plan, spec (agent-written, single purpose)

Why keep: universal. “Doc” is the common short form if a tool label ever needs it; “page” would clash with a frame’s page and a mockup’s page.

Play mode / prototype player

One name: Play mode

A chat’s app full screen with no canvas, for testing a flow or presenting. The docs call it both “Play mode” and “the prototype player”, and the menu says “Open in prototype player”.

Figma
Present, started from a ▶ play button
Framer, Webflow
Preview (taken by us for the running app)
Penpot
View mode

Why change: two names for one thing, and “prototype” is wrong for it: it plays the real app, while the thing people would call a prototype is a mockup. “Play mode” matches the ▶ and the ▶ in the tab title. Menu items would read “Open in Play mode”.

Knobs

Keep

Live controls an app declares (sliders, switches, colours) that sync to everyone on the canvas.

Storybook
Controls (its old addon was called Knobs)
Framer
property controls
Figma
variables, component properties

Why keep: “Controls” is the current standard, but we already use “control” for taking over a live frame (“Ask for control”, “Ana has control”) and for the player’s control bar. Knobs stays distinct and short.

Go live and Interact

Keep

Interact lets you click inside a frame or mockup. Go live moves it to one shared browser that everyone on the canvas sees.

Figma
Spotlight, follow (follow a person’s view)
Miro
Bring everyone to me
VS Code
Live Share

Why keep: the others share a person’s view; ours shares a page. “Go live” with a Live badge says that well, and “Interact” is the plain verb for clicking inside something on a canvas.
